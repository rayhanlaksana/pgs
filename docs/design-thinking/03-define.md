# Define

Following on from [02-empathy-pain-points.md](02-empathy-pain-points.md).

The pain points across user types cluster around a small number of underlying
gaps rather than five unrelated problems:

- No persistent record (Family, Paying Client)
- No structured way to flag/track status over time (Coach, Paying Client's "proof of value")
- Feedback not tied to a specific, referenceable moment (Injury client's form, Coach's video review)

## Overarching problem statement (POV)

A remote training client — whether family, friend, or paying client — needs a
persistent, shared record of what was said, prescribed, and done, and the
provider needs that record to actively surface who needs attention rather
than sit as passive history, because without both, continuity depends
entirely on memory and goodwill, and both erode with distance, time between
sessions, and the social discomfort of asking again.

This is the thread running through Family (no written record of cues), Close
Friend (no lightweight way to log, no accountability structure), Paying
Client (no steady proof-of-value, no written expectations), and Coach (no
single source of truth across clients, no at-a-glance view of who's lapsed).

## Supporting problem statements

These two don't fold into the record-keeping problem above — they're
distinct enough to need their own line.

### Injury/Rehab — safety, not visibility

An injured or physically limited client needs a way to report pain and
limitations honestly, in the moment, because the instinct to seem "fine"
combined with no live supervision means compensating form or overexertion
goes unnoticed until it's already caused a setback.

This is a judgment/disclosure problem, not a record-keeping one — a perfect
log of a session doesn't help if what got logged was already a downplayed
version of what happened.

### Coach video review — specificity, not existence

A coach needs a way to attach feedback to the exact rep or moment in a
submitted video, because unstructured review makes it too tedious to give
specific, actionable notes, so feedback defaults to generic, scattered, or
skipped.

Here the video already exists as a record — the gap is that feedback can't
be pinned to a moment within it, so "having a record" doesn't automatically
solve it.
