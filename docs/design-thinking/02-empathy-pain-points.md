# Empathy — Pain Points

Pain points per user type, following on from [01-empathy-user-types.md](01-empathy-user-types.md).

## 1. Family
- Forgets what was said between sessions — no written record to refer back to, so the same cues (e.g. "brace before you squat") have to be repeated in-person every time.
- Hesitant to ask for a reminder or to repeat something, out of embarrassment or not wanting to "bother" you since it's a family relationship, not a professional one.
- No clear sense of what today's session actually is before it starts — shows up without knowing what to expect or prepare for.

## 2. Close Friend
- Skips a session with no heads-up because there's low social cost to flaking on a friend compared to a paying client — no visible commitment or check-in structure to hold them accountable.
- Trains "whenever" on their own schedule, so progress/programming drifts out of sync with what was last discussed — you find out at the next call that they did something different or nothing at all.
- No lightweight way to log a session on their own — logging feels like effort, so it just doesn't happen, and consistency data is missing when you do check in.
- Their inconsistency costs you directly — you prep programming/cues for a session that gets skipped or done differently, and raising it feels awkward because correcting a friend risks the relationship in a way correcting a client doesn't.

## 3. Paying Client
- Long gaps between visible touchpoints make it unclear whether they're getting ongoing value for what they're paying — no steady drip of proof-of-attention (progress updates, acknowledgment of check-ins).
- Communication about expectations (what's included, pricing, response times) isn't written down anywhere both sides can refer to, so ambiguity turns into an awkward conversation later.
- Remote-only relationship makes it harder to establish trust early — first impressions rely entirely on how professional/organized the onboarding and first few interactions feel.

## 4. Injury / Rehab / Physical Limitation
- No live supervision means bad form (e.g. compensating around the injured area) can go uncaught until it's already caused pain or setback — you only find out after the fact, if at all.
- Client under-reports pain or limits in the moment (wanting to seem like they're "doing fine"), so the program doesn't get adjusted until the issue has already flared up.
- Coordinating with outside medical/PT guidance is informal (texts, verbal mentions) — details get lost, and there's no single place documenting what the medical restriction actually is.

## 5. Coach / Me (Provider)
- No at-a-glance view of who hasn't checked in this week — catching a lapsed client requires manually remembering or scrolling back through each person's history.
- Reviewing submitted form-check videos is tedious and unstructured — no consistent way to leave feedback tied to a specific rep/moment, so notes end up scattered across texts/messages.
- Tracking progress across multiple clients with different programs, injuries, and check-in cadences relies on memory or ad hoc notes rather than a single source of truth — easy to lose track of who needs attention next.
